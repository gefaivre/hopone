**Peak and Pace**, a run club in Brooklyn, New York, generated **$640,000 in revenue in 2025** (about £478,000), according to financial documents reviewed by CNBC and reported by IBTimes UK. Its founder, **Owen Akhibi Herrera**, is 27, and the club’s weekly Saturday runs are free.

## Where the money comes from

The runs do not make money. **Nearly all the revenue comes from ticketed parties**, priced at **$15 to $25**. The club also runs comedy nights, ski trips, hikes and yoga sessions. In September 2025 it held a 5K and cultural festival on Governors Island.

The reported profit margin is **15%**. On $640,000 of revenue, that is roughly **$96,000 in profit** (Hopone calculation).

## From 12 runners to 30,000 attendees

The growth has been fast:

- **12 people** came to the first run in 2024.
- About **700 runners** were coming by the club’s first summer.
- More than **30,000 people** attended its events in 2025.
- Herrera describes the wider community as “over 50,000 people.”

The market is crowded. New York has **about 200 run clubs**, and Strava data cited in the article puts the **growth in global run club participation at 59% in 2024**.

## Why it matters for the industry

Peak and Pace shows how a run club can make money: the run brings people in for free, and the revenue comes from the events around it. That is also why brands want access to these communities. For a sponsor, a club with tens of thousands of event attendees is a direct line to an engaged audience.

“I couldn’t care less about building the biggest run club in New York. I care about building one that actually means something,” Herrera wrote on LinkedIn.
