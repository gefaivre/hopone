Running the Irish Life Dublin Marathon will cost more in 2027. Organizers announced on September 29 that the **entry fee will rise from €110 to €130**, an **18% increase** and the first in five years.

## Why the price is going up

CEO Seán Ó Riain points to **cumulative inflation of 18% to 20%** over the past five years. He also cites higher costs for infrastructure, security, medical services and traffic management. The margin is thin: the race expects a **surplus of less than €70,000** this year.

“We want to retain that standard… and in order to retain that standard… that’s where we landed on with €130,” Ó Riain told The Irish Times.

## Demand is not the problem

The 2026 race drew a **record 47,000 applications** for a field capped at **22,500 runners**:

- **17,200** places went through the ballot, leaving 29,800 applicants unsuccessful.
- Another **5,300** entries came through charity, good-for-age, club and elite places.

On those numbers, the field’s maximum gross entry revenue rises from about €2.5 million to about **€2.9 million** (Hopone calculation: 22,500 × €130, before charity and complimentary places).

## The ballot also changes

The ballot fee drops from a non-refundable **€5** to **€4**, and unsuccessful applicants will now get it back. Under the old system, organizers collected **€149,000** in fees from unsuccessful applicants. The window for transferring entries is also extended to more than two months.

Prices for the organizer’s other 2027 races were also set: the Dublin Half Marathon costs **€55**, the Frank Duffy 10 Mile €40 and the Fingal 10k €35. The 2026 marathon takes place on October 25.
